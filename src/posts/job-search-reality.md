# 200+ Applications, 3 Interviews: One Student's Job Search Reality

Maya, a computer science student, tracked every single application during her job search.

The final count? **237 applications. 3 interviews. 1 offer.**

Here's her honest breakdown of what the job search actually looks like—not the LinkedIn highlight reel.

## The Numbers

Maya started applying in September of her senior year. By February, she had:

| Metric | Count |
|--------|-------|
| Total Applications | 237 |
| Responses (any kind) | 18 |
| Rejections | 15 |
| Interviews | 3 |
| Offers | 1 |
| **Response Rate** | **7.6%** |

**That means 92.4% of her applications went into a black hole.** No response. No acknowledgment. Nothing.

## What Actually Happened

### Month 1-2: Optimism

"I thought I'd apply to 20-30 companies and get a few interviews," Maya told us. "I had a 3.5 GPA, internship experience, relevant projects. I thought I was a strong candidate."

**Reality:** 45 applications. 0 responses.

### Month 3-4: Panic

"I started applying to everything. Jobs I was overqualified for. Jobs in cities I didn't want to live in. Anything tech-adjacent."

**Reality:** 89 more applications. 2 rejections. 1 interview (rejected after first round).

"Getting that first rejection email almost felt good," she said. "At least someone looked at my application."

### Month 5-6: Desperation

"My parents kept asking 'Have you found something yet?' My friends were posting job offers on LinkedIn. I felt like such a failure."

**Reality:** 103 more applications. Finally, 2 more interviews. 1 offer.

The offer came from a company she'd never heard of. A small tech firm, not a big name. Starting salary was $15k less than the "average CS grad" statistics promised.

**She took it.**

## Breaking Down the Applications

Maya tracked where she applied and what happened:

### By Company Size:

**FAANG + Big Tech (Google, Meta, Amazon, etc.):**
- Applications: 45
- Interviews: 0
- Offers: 0
- **Success rate: 0%**

**Mid-Size Tech Companies:**
- Applications: 125
- Interviews: 1
- Offers: 0
- **Success rate: 0%**

**Small Companies (<100 employees):**
- Applications: 67
- Interviews: 2
- Offers: 1
- **Success rate: 1.5%**

**Her advice:** "Don't ignore small companies. That's where I actually had a shot."

## What Made It Harder

### The ATS Black Hole

"I'd spend 2 hours customizing my resume and cover letter. Submit. Never hear back. It felt pointless."

**Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)** filter out resumes before humans see them. Maya suspects most of her applications never reached a real person.

### The "2-3 Years Experience Required"

"Every 'entry-level' job wanted 2-3 years experience. How am I supposed to get experience if no one will hire me?"

### The Ghosting

"Companies would say 'We'll get back to you in 2 weeks.' Then nothing. Forever. Just don't respond at all."

### The Fake Listings

"I applied to jobs that turned out to be collecting resumes for 'future openings.' They weren't actually hiring."

## What Helped (A Little)

### 1. Tracking Everything

Maya used a spreadsheet to track:
- Company name
- Position
- Date applied
- Date of follow-up
- Response status
- Interview dates
- Notes

"It helped me see I wasn't crazy. The numbers were just brutal for everyone."

### 2. Networking (But Not How You Think)

"'Networking' advice always felt fake. But I did reach out to alumni on LinkedIn. 2 of my 3 interviews came from those connections."

**What worked:**
- Short, specific messages
- Asking about their experience, not asking for jobs
- Following up with thank-you notes

**What didn't:**
- Generic "I'd love to connect" messages
- Asking strangers for referrals

### 3. Applying to "Unsexy" Jobs

"I wanted to work at a cool startup or big tech company. The job I got? A healthcare IT company. Not glamorous. But it's a real job with benefits."

### 4. Adjusting Resume for ATS

Maya learned to:
- Use standard formatting (no fancy designs)
- Include keywords from job description
- Save as .docx not PDF (some ATS can't read PDFs)
- Use standard section headers

[Build an ATS-friendly resume →](/resume-builder)

### 5. Actually Using Campus Resources

"I ignored career services for months. When I finally went, they helped me fix my resume and practice interviews. Should've done it sooner."

## The Mental Health Toll

"I cried in my car after every rejection email. My parents kept asking about my job search and I'd lie and say 'I'm waiting to hear back from a few places.'"

**What helped:**
- Therapy through campus counseling
- Talking to friends also job searching
- Taking breaks from applying
- Reminding herself the market was just hard

## What She Wants Other Students to Know

**"This is normal. The numbers are brutal for almost everyone."**

"LinkedIn makes it look like everyone has multiple offers. They don't. They just don't post about the 200 rejections first."

**"Small companies are underrated."**

"I wanted a big name on my resume. But my 'no-name' company gave me way more responsibility and mentorship than I'd get as intern #47 at a huge corporation."

**"Volume matters, but so does strategy."**

"I wasted time applying to jobs I had no shot at. I should've focused on companies actually hiring new grads and using my network."

**"It's not you. The system is broken."**

"Entry-level jobs requiring years of experience. ATS filtering out qualified candidates. Ghosting. It's not your fault the process sucks."

## Where Maya Is Now

Six months into the job, Maya says:

"I'm learning a ton. The company is small so I get to work on real projects, not just grunt work. The pay is okay—I'm not rich, but I'm not broke."

"Looking back, I'm glad I didn't give up. But man, those 6 months sucked."
