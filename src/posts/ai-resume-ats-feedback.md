You've stared at your resume for so long the words don't even look like words anymore.

You've moved bullets around. Changed the font. Debated whether "collaborated" or "worked with" sounds better. Asked a friend who said "looks good!" without really reading it.

And you still don't know if it's actually going to get past the automated filter that 75% of resumes never survive.

Here's something that might help: AI is genuinely good at this.

Not in a "here's a generic template" way. In a "this is exactly what's wrong with bullet three and here's how to fix it" way — if you know how to ask.

This guide will show you exactly how.

## First, What Is ATS and Why Should You Care?

Before we get into the how, a quick explainer.

ATS stands for **Applicant Tracking System**. It's software that companies use to automatically scan your resume before a human ever sees it. It's looking for specific keywords, formatting it can read, and structure it recognizes.

If your resume doesn't match what the ATS is looking for — even if you're a great fit for the job — it gets filtered out. Automatically. Without anyone reading it.

That's why a resume that looks beautiful in Canva might never get seen. That's why a resume that has all the right experience but uses "Worked alongside the team to execute deliverables" instead of the words in the job posting might get passed over.

The good news: once you understand the rules, you can fix them. And AI can help you do it fast.

## Before You Open the AI Tool

There's one thing that makes the difference between useful feedback and generic feedback:

**Have the job posting in front of you.**

Generic AI feedback will tell you "use strong action verbs" and "quantify your results." That's fine, but it's not targeted. When you paste in the actual job description alongside your resume, the AI can tell you specifically which keywords you're missing, which requirements you haven't addressed, and which parts of your experience are most relevant to *this* role.

Before you start, get these three things ready:

- **Your resume as plain text** — copy it out of your Word doc or PDF. Remove tables, columns, and text boxes if you have them. Plain text is what ATS actually reads, and it's what gives you the most accurate feedback.
- **The full job posting** — the whole thing, not just the title.
- **A few minutes** — good feedback takes a minute to generate. Don't rush it.

## The Prompts That Actually Work

Here are the exact prompts to use. Copy them, paste them into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, and add your resume and the job posting at the bottom.

### For Your Resume

This is the core prompt. Use it every time you're applying to a new role:

> *Review my resume for ATS optimization. Please check: formatting issues that would prevent ATS from reading this correctly, keywords from the job description that are missing from my resume, whether my bullets use strong action verbs and quantifiable results, whether my skills section is specific enough (e.g. "Python" vs "Python with NumPy and Pandas"), grammar and consistency issues, and the top 3 changes I should make. Be specific about what to change and why. Keep my authentic experience — just help me present it more effectively.*
>
> *[PASTE YOUR RESUME HERE]*
>
> *[PASTE THE JOB DESCRIPTION HERE]*

### For Your Cover Letter

Cover letters get scanned by ATS too. Use this prompt:

> *Review my cover letter for ATS optimization and overall quality. Check whether it uses keywords from the job posting, whether the company and exact role are named specifically, whether there are formatting issues that would hurt ATS parsing, whether it tells a story or just repeats my resume, and whether the tone is professional but still human. Give me the top 3 improvements.*
>
> *[PASTE YOUR COVER LETTER HERE]*
>
> *[PASTE THE JOB DESCRIPTION HERE]*

### For Keyword Gaps (Do This One First)

This is the most underused prompt — and honestly the most useful:

> *Compare my resume to this job description and identify the keyword gaps. List the keywords in the job description that are NOT in my resume, which of those I likely have experience with and should add, and how I could naturally work each one into my resume.*
>
> *[PASTE YOUR RESUME HERE]*
>
> *[PASTE THE JOB DESCRIPTION HERE]*

Run this one first, then use what it finds when you do the full resume review. You'll get much more targeted feedback.

## What to Ask About Specifically

Sometimes you want to dig into one specific area. Here are the questions worth asking:

**For ATS compatibility:**
- "Are there any formatting issues that would cause parsing errors?"
- "Are my section headers standard — EDUCATION, EXPERIENCE, SKILLS — or do I have creative names that ATS might not recognize?"
- "Are acronyms spelled out the first time they appear?"

**For keywords:**
- "What keywords from this job posting are missing from my resume?"
- "Does the language in my skills section match the language in this job posting?"

**For bullets:**
- "Do my bullets use strong action verbs and quantifiable results?"
- "Do my bullets follow the STAR format — Situation, Task, Action, Result?"
- "Which bullets are weakest and how would you rewrite them?"

**For cover letters:**
- "Does this cover letter mirror the language and keywords from the job posting?"
- "Does it sound tailored to this company, or does it sound generic?"
- "What's the one thing that would make this cover letter significantly stronger?"

## A Few Things Worth Knowing

**AI will not lie to make you feel better — but check its suggestions.**
AI feedback is usually specific and honest, which is exactly what you want. But always read the suggestions before you use them. If it rewrites a bullet to say you did something you didn't do, don't use it. Keep your experience accurate. The goal is better presentation, not fabrication.

**Different AI tools give slightly different feedback.**
ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are all good for this. If you get feedback from one and want a second opinion, paste the same prompt into another. Occasionally they'll catch different things.

**Do this for every job you apply to.**
Your resume shouldn't be one static document you send to 50 places. Each application deserves a version that's tuned to that specific job posting. It takes 10 extra minutes and makes a real difference.

**You still need to be the editor.**
AI gives you a starting point. You decide what to keep, what to change, and what doesn't sound like you. Your resume should still sound like a real person wrote it — because a real person is going to be interviewed based on it.

## One Last Thing

Getting your resume past ATS isn't about gaming the system or being fake.

It's about making sure that when you apply for something you're genuinely qualified for, your resume actually gets read by a human who can see that.

You put in the work. You have the experience. This is just making sure the door opens.

Use the tools. Get the feedback. Apply.
