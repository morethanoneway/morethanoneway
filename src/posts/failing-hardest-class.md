# He Failed His Hardest Class - Here's What Happened Next

We recently spoke with Jordan, a college junior who shared his experience failing what was supposed to be his major's "core class."

You know that feeling when you check your grade and your stomach drops?

Yeah. Jordan failed his sophomore year.

Everyone else seemed to get it. The professor would explain concepts, and his classmates would nod knowingly. Jordan would sit there completely lost, pretending to understand while his anxiety skyrocketed.

## The Breaking Point

After bombing the midterm (32%), Jordan seriously considered switching majors entirely. His parents had sacrificed so much for his education. His high school teachers had written glowing recommendations about his potential. And here he was, failing.

He felt like a fraud.

"I thought I was the only one," Jordan told us. "Everyone else seemed fine. I felt so stupid."

## What Actually Helped Him

Here's what Jordan wishes someone had told him on day one:

### 1. He Finally Talked to the Professor

Jordan avoided office hours for weeks because he was embarrassed. When he finally went, his professor said something that changed everything:

**"About 30% of students struggle with this class. You're not behind - you just learn differently."**

The professor recommended:
- Breaking problems into smaller steps
- Drawing diagrams for every concept
- Teaching concepts back to explain understanding
- Using different learning resources (not just the textbook)

"I thought professors would judge me," Jordan said. "Instead, mine actually wanted to help."

### 2. He Got Accommodations Through Disability Services

Jordan has ADHD but never used accommodations before - he didn't want to feel "different." 

Turns out, extra time on exams wasn't "cheating" - it was leveling the playing field so he could actually show what he knew.

"I spent years thinking I just wasn't smart enough," Jordan explained. "Turns out my brain just works differently, and that's okay."

**If you have ADHD, anxiety, depression, or any condition affecting your learning:** Disability services exists for a reason. Use it.

[Learn more about accommodations →](/study-resources)

### 3. He Found a Study Group That Actually Helped

Not the "let's copy homework" kind. The "let's explain concepts to each other" kind.

Jordan's study group met twice a week, worked through practice problems together, and most importantly: admitted when they didn't understand something. No pretending.

"Having people to struggle WITH made all the difference," he said.

### 4. He Stopped Comparing Himself to "Naturals"

Some students breeze through difficult classes. Good for them. But most skills aren't talents you're born with - they're built through repetition and failure.

Jordan's friend who "got it immediately"? Turns out they'd been studying the subject informally for years. Jordan was brand new to it. Of course they had a head start.

"I realized comparing myself to people with totally different backgrounds was pointless," he said.

### 5. He Retook the Class (And Passed With a B+)

Retaking wasn't failure. It was giving himself the time he needed to actually learn instead of just survive.

Second time through:
- He understood the "why" not just the "how"
- Office hours felt normal, not scary
- He could help other struggling students
- The material actually made sense
- He didn't feel behind anymore

"The second time, I wasn't panicking," Jordan explained. "I was actually learning."

## Resources That Saved Jordan

**Free tutoring:**
- Campus learning center (free peer tutoring)
- Professor office hours
- Online resources specific to his subject
- YouTube tutorials (breaking down concepts differently)

**Mental health:**
- Campus counseling (when he was spiraling)
- ADHD coaching through disability services
- Crisis support when thoughts got dark

## What Jordan Would Tell His Past Self

**"Failing one class doesn't define your potential,"** Jordan told us. **"It doesn't mean you're not 'cut out' for your major. It doesn't mean you wasted your parents' money. It doesn't mean you should quit."**

"It means you're learning something hard, and that takes time."

Jordan pointed out that some of the most successful people in his field failed classes. They just didn't quit.

## The Truth About Failing in College

Here's what most students don't realize: **failing is incredibly common.**

Studies show that:
- About 30-40% of college students fail or withdraw from at least one class
- The majority of students who graduate retook at least one course
- Most professionals can point to a class they struggled with or failed

But here's the problem: **nobody talks about it.**

Everyone posts the acceptance letters, the job offers, the graduation photos. Nobody posts the failed exams, the withdrawal forms, the "I don't know if I can do this" moments.

So students like Jordan think they're the only ones failing. They're not.

## Final Thoughts

Jordan is now a senior, doing well in his classes, and planning for graduation.

"I used to think failing meant I was a failure," he said. "Now I know failing is just part of learning. Everyone's timeline is different."

**His advice to struggling students:**

"Talk to your professor. Get help early. Use every resource available. Stop comparing yourself to people with different circumstances. And remember: one bad grade doesn't define you."
