# When Family Falls Short: Building the Support You Deserve

Not every college student has family support.

Maybe your family doesn't accept who you are. Maybe they don't understand your path. Maybe you're estranged, or navigating college completely alone.

**This doesn't mean you have to do it alone.**

There are actual organizations—run by real parents, grandparents, and adults—who volunteer to be chosen family for students who need them.

## What is "Chosen Family"?

Chosen family means people who aren't related to you by blood but show up for you like family should.

They're not trying to replace your biological family. They're filling a gap when your family can't or won't be there.

**Here's what chosen family can look like:**
- A supportive mom figure who checks in on you
- A dad who shows up to your graduation when yours won't
- Grandparents who send you care packages during finals
- Adults who are genuinely proud of you
- People who accept you exactly as you are

## Organizations That Connect Students with Chosen Family

### **1. PFLAG (Parents, Families, and Friends of Lesbians and Gays)**

**What they do:**  
The largest organization supporting LGBTQ+ people and their families. They have local chapters across the U.S. with parents who want to support LGBTQ+ students whose own families rejected them.

**Who it's for:**  
LGBTQ+ students who lack family support

**What you can get:**
- Connection with supportive parents in your area
- Support groups (virtual and in-person)
- One-on-one mentorship
- Help navigating tough situations
- Someone to talk to during holidays

**How to access:**  
Visit [pflag.org](https://pflag.org) and find your local chapter. Many chapters specifically connect with college students.

**Cost:** Free

---

### **2. Free Mom Hugs**

**What they do:**  
An organization of affirming moms who show up for LGBTQ+ people who need maternal support. They literally show up at Pride events, college campuses, and community events offering hugs, support, and "mom" advice.

**Who it's for:**  
LGBTQ+ people (especially youth and young adults) whose moms aren't supportive

**What you can get:**
- Literal hugs from affirming moms
- Emotional support
- "Mom" advice and encouragement
- Someone to talk to about life, relationships, struggles
- Support during coming out or transitions

**How to access:**  
Visit [freemomhugs.org](https://freemomhugs.org)  
Follow them on social media to find when they're at events near you  
Many chapters do campus visits

**Cost:** Free

**Real impact:**  
"I hadn't hugged my mom in 3 years. A Free Mom Hugs volunteer hugged me at Pride and I cried for 20 minutes. She just held me and said 'I'm proud of you.' I needed that so badly." —College student

---

### **3. Free Dad Hugs**

**What they do:**  
Same concept as Free Mom Hugs, but dads. Supportive father figures for LGBTQ+ people who lack that in their lives.

**Who it's for:**  
LGBTQ+ people whose dads aren't supportive or present

**What you can get:**
- Father figure support
- Encouragement and pride
- Someone to talk to about life decisions
- "Dad" advice

**How to access:**  
Visit [freedadhugs.org](https://freedadhugs.org)  
Follow on social media for events

**Cost:** Free

---

### **4. The Trevor Project**

**What they do:**  
While primarily a crisis intervention service, Trevor also connects LGBTQ+ youth with resources and support networks.

**Who it's for:**  
LGBTQ+ young people (ages 13-24)

**What you can get:**
- 24/7 crisis support (call, text, or chat)
- Connection to local LGBTQ+ resources
- Peer support communities
- Mental health resources

**How to access:**  
**Call:** 1-866-488-7386  
**Text:** Text START to 678-678  
**Chat:** [thetrevorproject.org/get-help](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/get-help)

**Cost:** Free and confidential

---

### **5. Campus LGBTQ+ Resource Centers**

**What they do:**  
Most colleges have LGBTQ+ centers that offer more than just event planning. Many have mentor programs connecting students with supportive adults.

**Who it's for:**  
LGBTQ+ students at your college

**What you can get:**
- Peer support groups
- Faculty/staff mentors
- "Chosen family" dinner programs
- Safe spaces on campus
- Connection to off-campus resources

**How to access:**  
Search "[Your University] LGBTQ+ Resource Center" or check your student services website

**Cost:** Free (included in tuition)

---

### **6. First-Generation Student Support Programs**

**What they do:**  
While not specifically for family rejection, first-gen programs help students whose families can't guide them through college.

**Who it's for:**  
Students whose parents didn't attend college

**What you can get:**
- Mentorship from faculty/staff
- Peer support groups
- Academic guidance your family can't provide
- Financial literacy workshops
- Professional development

**How to access:**  
Search "[Your University] First-Generation Student Programs" or ask your academic advisor

**Cost:** Free

## How to Actually Use These Resources

### **If You're Nervous About Reaching Out:**

**Start small:**
- Follow organizations on social media first
- Attend one virtual meeting (camera off if you want)
- Send an email instead of calling
- Bring a friend for support

**You don't have to share everything:**
- You can just listen at first
- You decide what to share and when
- These aren't therapy sessions—they're support

**It's okay to try multiple organizations:**
- Not every group will feel right
- Some connections click better than others
- Keep trying until you find your people

### **What to Expect:**

**From PFLAG:**  
Usually support group meetings (virtual or in-person). You introduce yourself with whatever you're comfortable sharing. People share struggles and support each other. Very judgment-free.

**From Free Mom/Dad Hugs:**  
At events: They approach you, offer a hug, and chat if you want. Some become ongoing connections.  
Online: They respond to messages, offer encouragement, and check in.

**From Campus Resources:**  
Usually an intake meeting to understand what you need, then they connect you with relevant programs/people.

## For Specific Situations

### **"My family disowned me for being LGBTQ+"**

→ PFLAG, Free Mom Hugs, Free Dad Hugs, Trevor Project, Campus LGBTQ+ Center

### **"I'm the first in my family to go to college and they can't help me"**

→ First-Gen Student Programs, Campus Advising, PFLAG (if also LGBTQ+)

### **"My family is unsupportive but not completely absent"**

→ Campus counseling, PFLAG support groups, peer support communities

### **"I have no family at all"**

→ All of the above, plus talk to financial aid about independent student status and emergency housing during breaks

## Building Your Own Support Network

While these organizations help, you can also build chosen family from:

**On campus:**
- Trusted professors who believe in you
- Campus staff who check in
- Friends who show up consistently
- Roommates who accept you
- Student organization communities

**Off campus:**
- Coworkers who are supportive
- Neighbors who care
- Online communities (Discord, Reddit, etc.)
- Volunteer groups
- Faith communities (if you want that)

**"Family" isn't about blood. It's about who shows up.**

## During Holidays & Breaks

**Holidays are especially hard without family support.**

**Options:**
- **Friendsgiving:** Spend holidays with friends
- **Campus programs:** Some schools offer housing/meals for students who can't go home
- **PFLAG families:** Some specifically invite students for holidays
- **Volunteer:** Many find purpose in volunteering at shelters/soup kitchens during holidays

**You're allowed to create new traditions.**

## You Deserve Support

**If your family isn't there for you, it's not because you're unlovable.**

Sometimes families fail us. Sometimes they can't handle their own biases, beliefs, or limitations. Sometimes they just don't understand.

**That's on them, not you.**

You deserve people who:
- Accept you as you are
- Show up when you need them
- Are proud of you
- Support your path
- Choose you

**These organizations exist because you're not alone in this.**

Thousands of students navigate college without family support. Thousands have found chosen family and made it through.

You can too.
