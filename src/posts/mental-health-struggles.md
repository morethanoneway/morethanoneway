# When Everyone Seems Fine But You're Drowning

Everyone else seems to have it together.

They're going to parties. Getting good grades. Looking happy in their Instagram stories.

Meanwhile, you're struggling to get out of bed. Skipping classes because the thought of leaving your room feels impossible. Wondering if you're the only one falling apart.

**You're not.**

## The Lie We Believe

**The lie:** "Everyone else is fine. I'm the only one struggling."

**The truth:** Most college students are struggling. They're just hiding it better.

Studies show:
- **60% of college students** report overwhelming anxiety
- **40% experience depression** so severe they can't function
- **1 in 3 students** seriously consider dropping out due to mental health

But here's what happens: Everyone posts the good days. Nobody posts the breakdowns.

So you think you're alone. You're not.

## What Depression Actually Looks Like in College

It's not always crying in bed (though sometimes it is).

Depression in college often looks like:

**Academically:**
- Can't focus on readings
- Staring at assignments for hours, getting nothing done
- Missing classes because you can't make yourself go
- Grades dropping even though you're "trying"

**Socially:**
- Canceling plans at the last minute
- Feeling lonely even when surrounded by people
- Too exhausted to text back
- Watching everyone else have fun and feeling nothing

**Physically:**
- Sleeping 12+ hours or barely sleeping at all
- Eating too much or forgetting to eat
- Always tired no matter how much you rest
- Body just feels... heavy

**Emotionally:**
- Everything feels pointless
- Can't remember the last time you felt genuinely happy
- Numb instead of sad
- "I don't care" about things you used to love

## What Anxiety Actually Looks Like in College

It's not just "being stressed about exams" (though that's part of it).

Anxiety in college often looks like:

**Before class:**
- Heart racing for no reason
- Convincing yourself everyone's judging you
- Spiral of "what ifs"
- Physical symptoms (nausea, dizziness, shaking)

**During assignments:**
- Paralyzed by perfectionism
- Can't start because you're scared it won't be good enough
- Checking and rechecking everything
- Panic attacks when you realize you're behind

**Socially:**
- Overanalyzing every interaction
- Convinced people secretly hate you
- Avoiding situations that might be awkward
- Exhausted from pretending to be okay

**Physically:**
- Constant tension in shoulders/neck
- Trouble breathing deeply
- Can't turn your brain off
- Feeling "on edge" all the time

## When It's More Than "Just Stress"

**"Normal" college stress:**
- You're worried about specific things (exam, deadline, presentation)
- Manageable with time management and breaks
- Improves after the stressful event passes
- You can still function day-to-day

**When to get help:**
- Can't function in daily life (skipping classes, not eating, can't get out of bed)
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
- Using substances to cope
- Friends/family expressing concern
- Been feeling this way for weeks/months, not just days
- Physical symptoms (chest pain, can't breathe, frequent panic attacks)

**If you're thinking "I don't know if I'm bad enough to get help"—that thought itself means you should get help.**

## What Actually Helps

### 1. Campus Counseling

Most colleges offer free counseling. Yes, there's usually a waitlist. Call anyway.

**What to say when you call:**
"Hi, I'm a student and I'm struggling with my mental health. I'd like to make an appointment."

That's it. You don't need to explain everything. They'll ask questions.

**If there's a wait:** Ask about crisis appointments, groups, or drop-in hours. Many campuses have short-term options while you wait for regular counseling.

### 2. Crisis Resources (Use These ANY TIME)

**988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**
- Call or text: 988
- 24/7, free, confidential
- You don't have to be actively suicidal to call

**Crisis Text Line**
- Text HOME to 741741
- 24/7, free
- If you prefer texting

[More crisis resources →](/crisis)

### 3. Accommodations

If depression/anxiety is affecting your academics:

**Disability services can provide:**
- Extended test time
- Excused absences for mental health
- Deadline extensions
- Reduced course load
- Note-taking support

**You don't need to be "disabled enough."** If it's impacting your school work, you qualify.

### 4. Medical Leave (Yes, This Is an Option)

If you're really drowning: taking a semester off is okay.

**Medical leave means:**
- You can return when you're ready
- Often doesn't affect financial aid
- No "W" grades on your transcript
- Gives you time to actually get better

**Taking time off ≠ giving up. Sometimes it's the smartest thing you can do.**

### 5. Medication (It's Okay)

"I don't want to be on pills" is a valid feeling.

But also: **If you had diabetes, you'd take insulin.** Mental health medication works the same way.

Campus health centers can prescribe:
- Antidepressants
- Anti-anxiety medication
- ADHD medication
- Sleep aids

Not everyone needs medication. But if therapy alone isn't enough, it's worth considering.

## Things That Help (Even Though They Sound Cliché)

**Moving your body:**
Not "work out every day." Even a 5-minute walk helps. Something about movement shifts your brain.

**Routine:**
When everything feels chaotic, structure helps. Even just: wake up at the same time, eat one real meal, do one small task.

**Telling someone:**
"I'm not doing well" to ONE person. A friend, roommate, parent, professor. You don't have to suffer silently.

**Small wins:**
Showered today? That counts. Ate something? Win. Went to one class? Victory. Lower your bar for success.

## What Doesn't Help (But People Say Anyway)

❌ "Just think positive"  
❌ "Everyone gets stressed in college"  
❌ "Have you tried yoga?"  
❌ "It could be worse"  
❌ "You just need to sleep more"

**These aren't helpful. Your feelings are valid.**

## You're Not Broken

Depression and anxiety aren't character flaws.

They're not signs you're weak or can't handle college.

**They're medical conditions. Treatable ones.**

You wouldn't tell someone with a broken leg to "just walk it off." Don't tell yourself to "just get over it."
