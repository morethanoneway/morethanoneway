There's something about hitting the middle of summer that makes college feel very real, very fast.

If you just finished your junior year, you're probably starting to feel the weight of what comes next. If you're about to start your freshman year, the excitement and the dread are probably showing up at the same time. Both are completely normal. Neither means something is wrong with you.

## If you're heading into freshman year

Nobody really prepares you for how many things hit at once.

You're excited. You're nervous. You've been looking forward to this for years and somehow it still doesn't feel real. You're going to be living with a stranger, figuring out a new campus, managing your own schedule for the first time, and doing it all while trying to look like you have it together.

Some things that are completely normal and nobody warns you about:

Homesickness hits people who didn't expect it. Even if you wanted to leave. Even if things at home were complicated. Missing familiar things is not weakness, it's just human.

Making friends takes longer than a week. The people who seem to have found their group on day one usually haven't. Give it a semester.

Freedom is harder than it looks. No one telling you to go to class, do your laundry, or eat something other than dining hall pizza. That adjustment is real and it catches a lot of people off guard.

Getting involved early matters more than most people realize. Clubs, organizations, campus jobs — that's where you meet people and build the resume at the same time.

## If you're heading into your senior year

The pressure of senior year is its own thing entirely.

Everyone around you seems to have a plan. They know what they want, where they're applying, what they're doing after graduation. You're still figuring out what you actually want to do with your life and graduation is coming whether you're ready or not.

A few honest things worth knowing:

Not having a plan at the start of senior year is more common than it looks. Most people are performing confidence they don't have.

The job search takes longer than you think. The average is six to eight months. Starting early matters but panicking in October because you don't have an offer yet is not necessary.

Your major does not lock you in. Whatever you studied, there are adjacent careers that value the same skills — often with less competition than the obvious paths.

It's okay to not know exactly what you want yet. What matters is starting to explore, not having the answer.

## Wherever you are

Mid-summer is a strange in-between. Summer isn't over but the school year is close enough to feel real. Whatever you're carrying into this next semester — nerves, excitement, dread, hope, some combination of all of it — you're not the only one.
